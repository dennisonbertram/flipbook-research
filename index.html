Can Generated Pages Come Alive?

We explored a simple dream: take an AI-generated page, like a field guide or illustrated explainer, and make it feel alive without losing the page itself.

The question was not just “can we animate an image?” That is already possible. The harder question was whether a page can move while still remaining a page.

A page is different from a normal picture. If a generated landscape changes a branch, the image may still work. If a generated page changes a word, a label, a diagram, or the relationship between sections, the meaning can change. That makes “living pages” a harder problem than ordinary generated video.

We tried two broad approaches. One asked video models to animate the whole page. The other tried smaller, more controlled page motion: subtle movement, layout experiments, and lightweight animation that kept the original page closer to the center.

What We Saw

The best examples were not the cleanest successes. They were the examples that made the tradeoff visible: beauty versus control, motion versus meaning, atmosphere versus readability.

What We Learned

The main lesson is simple: video models are powerful motion engines, but they are not yet reliable page keepers.

They are good at atmosphere, texture, and plausible movement. They are much weaker at preserving exact words, diagrams, margins, and layout relationships. That means they can be useful for enhancement, but risky as the thing that owns the page.

The more promising near-term path is hybrid. Keep the page itself as the reference. Add controlled motion immediately. Then, for pages that can tolerate it, try richer generated video in the background and only use it when it still feels like the same page.

What This Means

A useful product is possible before the full research dream is solved. Generated reports, illustrated essays, educational pages, product explainers, and field-guide-style pages can already become more alive through careful, controlled motion.

But true generated re-layout is still open. To get there, the system needs to understand the page as something structured: text, diagrams, illustrations, locked regions, movable regions, and meaning. The model cannot treat the whole thing as one picture and hope the important details survive.
